TYPICAL PEDON: Fushatchee mucky peat in an oligohaline marsh at sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Oese--0 to 35 centimeters; very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) broken face and rubbed mucky peat; 50 percent fibers, 25 percent rubbed; von Post H4; many very fine and fine roots, and few medium roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.9 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.0), very strongly acid (pH 4.8) oxidized pH; gradual boundary (0 to 35 centimeters thick)

 Cseg1--91 to 147 centimeters; dark gray (10YR 4/1) sandy clay loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; very fluid; few very fine and fine roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; very slightly saline (2.0 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.2), very strongly acid (pH 4.5) oxidized pH; clear boundary

 Cseg--147 to 200 centimeters; dark gray (2.5Y 4/1) loamy fine sand; single grain; nonsticky, nonplastic; nonfluid; few very fine roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; very slightly saline (2.3 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.2), very strongly acid (pH 4.5) oxidized pH

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Blackrail, Cottonmouth, Haulover, Millender, and Wulfert soils.
Blackrail soils--formed in sandy alluvium and estuarine deposits and do not contain hypersulfidic materials within 100 centimeters of the surface.
Cottonmouth soils--have greater thickness of mucky peat or hemic horizons compared to muck or sapric horizons and generally occur in higher salinity portions of the estuary.
Haulover soils--formed in fine-loamy estuarine deposits and have histic epipedons.
Millender soils--formed in fine-loamy estuarine deposits and do not contain hypersulfidic materials within 50 centimeters of the mineral soil surface.
Wulfert soils--formed in woody and herbaceous organic material and have a sandy or sandy-skeletal particle size class. They also have up to 20 percent shell fragments in their sandy horizons. 

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Very low to moderately low
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Very frequent, for brief durations.  Fushatchee soils are flooded by wind and lunar tides, and riverine flooding. Inundation depths on Fushatchee soils are irregular. 

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Fushatchee soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Fushatchee soils located on oligohaline tidal marshes are typically dominated by black needlerush (Juncus roemerianus), sawgrass (Cladium jamaicense), and to a limited extent common reed (Phragmites australis). Tidal forest floodplains and transitional freshwater marshes are equally interspersed with bald cypress (Taxodium disitichum), white tupelo (Nyssa ogeche), saw palmetto (Serenoa repens), cottonmouth grass (Phanopyrum gymnocarpon), and sawgrass.

REMARKS:
The series is named after the Fushatchee Band of Muscogee Creek Native Americans who relocated to the panhandle of Florida after the Red Stick War. Fushatchee soils were previously mapped as the Bohicket, Brickyard, Chowan, Kenner, Maurepas, and the Tisonia soil series in Florida soil surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Hemic soil materials--The zone from 0 to 35 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is greater than one-sixth but less than three-fourths of the original soil volume.
Sapric soil materials--The zone from 35 to 91 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is less than one-sixth of the original soil volume.
Hyposulfidic materials--The zone from 0 to 35 centimeters and 91 to 200 centimeters has an initial pH that decreases by 1 or more units but remains at a value greater than 4.0 after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Hypersulfidic materials--The zone from 35 to 91 centimeters has an initial pH that decreases by 1 or more units to a value of 4.0 or less after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Fluidity--The zone from 91 to 147 centimeters has a fluidity class of very fluid.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
