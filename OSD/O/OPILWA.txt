TAXONOMIC CLASS: Fine-loamy, mixed, active, nonacid, hyperthermic Thapto-Histic Hydraquents

TYPICAL PEDON: Opilwa fine sandy loam on a broad floodplain, 0.3 meters above sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Aseg--0 to 15 centimeters; dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2) fine sandy loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; very fluid; many very fine and fine, and common medium roots; no sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; strong effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.8 dS/m); moderately acid (pH 5.9), very strongly acid (pH 4.8) oxidized pH; clear boundary (5 to 30 centimeters thick)

 2Oaseb1--68 to 105 centimeters; very dark brown (10YR 2/2) broken face and rubbed muck; 40 percent fibers, 10 percent rubbed; von Post H8; many very fine, fine, and medium roots; 10 percent wood fragments; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; strong effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.9 dS/m); neutral (pH 6.6), extremely acid (pH 4.3) oxidized pH; clear boundary

 2Oaseb2--105 to 146 centimeters; black (10YR 2/1) broken face and rubbed muck; 50 percent fibers, 10 percent rubbed; von Post H9; many very fine and common fine roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (1.0 dS/m); neutral (pH 6.7), extremely acid (pH 4.1) oxidized pH; gradual boundary

 2Oaseb3--146 to 203 centimeters; black (2.5Y 2.5/1) broken face and very dark brown (10YR 2/2) rubbed muck; 70 percent fibers, 15 percent rubbed; von Post H7; many very fine roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; strong effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; very slightly saline (2.5 dS/m); neutral (pH 6.6), ultra acid (pH 3.4) oxidized pH

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Maurepas, Romalea, and Yent soils.
Maurepas soils--formed in woody organic materials and have greater than 130 centimeters of soil organic materials at the surface.
Romalea soils--formed in fine-loamy alluvium and estuarine deposits and do not contain 40 centimeters or more of buried organic soil materials.
Yent soils--formed in herbaceous and woody organic soil materials over loamy estuarine deposits and have greater than 40 centimeters of sapric soil material at the surface. 

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Moderately low to moderately high
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Frequent, for long durations. Opilwa soils dominantly experience riverine flooding, but soils mapped closer to estuaries experience some level of lunar tidal flooding. 

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Opilwa soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Bald cypress (Taxodium disitichum), white tupelo (Nyssa ogeche), saw palmetto (Serenoa repens), cottonmouth grass (Phanopyrum gymnocarpon), and interspersed sawgrass (Cladium jamaicense).

REMARKS:
The series is named after the anglicized Muscogee Creek word for "swamp" or "wetland". It is the root word for tupelo trees "ito opilwa" found across these soils. Opilwa soils were previously mapped as the Brickyard, Chowan, and Kenner soil series in Florida soil surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Ochric epipedon--The zone from 0 to 15 centimeters.
Fluidity--The zone from 0 to 68 centimeters has a fluidity class of moderately fluid to very fluid.
Lithologic discontinuity--The zone from 68 to 203 centimeters formed in woody organic materials.
Sapric soil materials--The zone from 68 to 203 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is less than one-sixth of the original soil volume.
Hyposulfidic materials--The zone form 0 to 146 centimeters has an initial pH that decreased by 1 or more units but remains at a value greater than 4.0 after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Hypersulfidic materials--The zone from 146 to 203 centimeters has an initial pH that decreases by 1 or more units to a value of 4.0 or less after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
