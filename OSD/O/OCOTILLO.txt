OCOTILLO SERIES

The Ocotillo series consists of very deep, well drained soils that formed in alluvium from igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary rocks. Ocotillo soils are on fan remnants and have slopes of 0 to 10 percent. The mean annual precipitation is about 105 mm and the mean annual temperature is about 23 degrees C.                                                                                     

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Coarse-loamy, mixed, superactive, hyperthermic Typic Haplargids

TYPICAL PEDON: Ocotillo loamy sand on a slope of 3 percent under a cover of teddybear cholla, burrobush, creosote bush, ocotillo, and California barrel cactus. (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted). 

  A--0 to 3 cm; light yellowish brown (10YR 6/4) loamy sand, yellowish brown (10YR 5/4) moist; moderate very thick platy structure; very friable, slightly hard, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine roots throughout; common very fine vesicular pores; 3 percent gravel; strong effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 7.9); abrupt smooth boundary. (2 to 5 cm thick) 

  AB--3 to 20 cm; light brown (7.5YR 6/4) gravelly coarse sand, brown (7.5YR 5/4) moist; weak medium granular structure; very friable, soft, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine and few fine roots throughout; common irregular pores; 20 percent gravel; slight effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 7.9); clear wavy boundary. (15 to 26 cm thick)

  Bt--20 to 50 cm; brown (7.5YR 5/4) coarse sandy loam, brown (7.5YR 4/4) moist; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable, slightly hard, slightly sticky, slightly plastic; common very fine and few fine roots throughout; common irregular pores; 30 percent faint brown (7.5YR 4/3), dry, clay bridges between sand grains; 10 percent gravel; very slight effervescence; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); clear wavy boundary. (20 to 54 cm thick) 

  BC--50 to 62 cm; brown (7.5YR 5/4) gravelly loamy coarse sand, brown (7.5YR 4/4) moist; weak medium subangular blocky structure; very friable, soft, nonsticky, nonplastic; few very fine and fine roots throughout; common irregular pores; 2 percent cobbles; 12 percent gravel; strong effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.0); clear wavy boundary. (12 to 52 cm thick)

  C--62 to 200 cm; pale brown (10YR 6/3) very gravelly sand, brown (10YR 5/3) moist; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; many interstitial pores; 50 percent gravel; noneffervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8) 

TYPE LOCATION: San Diego County, California; about 2.7 kilometers (1.7 miles) north of the intersection of Old Borrego Valley Road and Highway 78 and 1.1 kilometers (0.7 miles) due west. USGS 7.5 minute topographic quadrangle: Borrego Sink, California. WGS84 33.158333 latitude and -116.273611 longitude; UTM Zone 11, 3669080 meters N and 567717 meters E NAD83.

RANGE IN CHARACTERISTICS:
Soil Temperature: Hyperthermic temperature regime. Mean annual soil temperature at 50 cm depth is 23 degrees C, and the difference between mean summer and mean winter soil temperature is greater than 6 degrees C. 

Soil Moisture: Typic-aridic soil moisture regime.  Usually dry, moist in some parts for short periods during winter and early spring and for 10 to 20 days cumulative between July and September following summer convection storms.

COMPETING SERIES:
These are the Rubylee soils:
Rubylee soils have 8 to 15 percent clay above the argillic horizon and secondary silica accumulation in the lower part of the subsoil. 

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING: 
Landscape: fan piedmonts
Landform: fan remnants
Parent material: alluvium derived from igneous, metamorphic, and sedimentary rocks
Slope: 0 to 10 percent
Elevation: 85 to 550 meters
Mean annual precipitation: 90 to 160 mm
Mean January temperature: 13 degrees C
Mean July temperature: 31 degrees C
Mean annual air temperature: 19 to 23 degrees C
Frost-free days: 305 to 360 days

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Buzzardsprings, Carsitas, Goldrose, and Vint soils:
Buzzardsprings are somewhat excessively drained, do not have an argillic horizon, and are found on fan remnants.
Carsitas are somewhat excessively drained, have a sandy particle-size control section, and are found on alluvial fans and drainageways.
Goldrose are somewhat excessively drained, have a sandy particle-size control section with have at least one horizon averaging greater than 35 percent rock fragments, and are found on alluvial fans and drainageways.
Vint are somewhat excessively drained, have a sandy particle-size control section, and are found on floodplains.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTVITIY: Well drained; moderately high to high saturated hydraulic conductivity.

USE AND VEGETATION: This soil is used for recreation and wildlife habitat. Vegetation is dominantly creosote bush, burrobush, asian mustard, ocotillo, big galleta, barrel cactus, teddybear cholla, pencil cholla, silver cholla, and white rhatany. 

DISTRIBTUION AND EXTENT: The soils are of small extent and are mapped in the lower Colorado Desert of California. MLRA: 40 -- Sonoran Desert                                        

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: Davis, California.

SERIES ESTABLISHED: San Diego County, California, Anza-Borrego Area Soil Survey, 2011. The soil is named after the community of Ocotillo Wells.
