MARYS SERIES

Depth class: very deep 
Drainage class: well drained 
Parent material: sandy and gravelly alluvium 
Landscape: mountains 
Landform: flood plains, mountain valleys 
Slope: 2 to 12 percent 
Mean annual precipitation: about 1870 mm (72.9 in) 
Mean annual air temperature: about 3 degrees C (37.4 F) 
Frost-free period: 130 to 155 days 

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Sandy or sandy-skeletal, mixed Typic Cryofluvents

TYPICAL PEDON: Marys cobbles in a maritime forest community on a northwest facing linear, linear, gravelly, high flood-plain step on a slope of 6 percent at an elevation of 23m (75 ft).

     Oi--0 to 6 cm (0 to 2 in); slightly decomposed plant material, black (7.5YR 2.5/1) moist, dark gray (7.5YR 4/1) dry; many very fine and many fine roots throughout; extremely acid (pH 4.2); clear smooth boundary.

     Oe--6 to 12 cm (2 to 5 in); moderately decomposed plant material, very dark brown (10YR 2/2) moist, dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2) dry; many very fine and fine roots throughout; extremely acid (pH 4.2); clear smooth boundary. 

     C1--12 to 18 cm (5 to 7 in); cobbles; single grain; common very fine and fine roots throughout; very fine and fine interstitial pores; 10 percent subangular stones by volume, 22 percent subangular gravel by volume, 60 percent subangular cobbles by volume; strongly acid (pH 5.4); clear smooth boundary.

     C2--18 to 34 cm (7 to 13 in); very gravelly sandy loam, very dark grayish brown (2.5Y 3/2) broken face moist, grayish brown (2.5Y 5/2) broken face dry; weak medium subangular blocky structure; soft, very friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine and fine roots throughout; very fine and fine interstitial and very fine dendritic tubular pores; 10 percent rounded cobbles by volume, 25 percent rounded gravel by volume; strongly acid (pH 5.4); clear smooth boundary. 

     C3--34 to 200 cm (13 to 79 in); extremely gravelly sand, very dark grayish brown (2.5Y 3/2) broken face moist, grayish brown (2.5Y 5/2) broken face dry; weak medium subangular blocky structure; loose, loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine roots throughout; very fine and fine interstitial pores; 10 percent rounded cobbles by volume, 75 percent rounded gravel by volume; strongly acid (pH 5.4).

COMPETING SERIES: These are the Army (AK), Chenega (AK), Hollow (AK), Ironcreek (AK), Kidazqeni (AK), Malmesbury (AK), Shovelcreek (AK), Stratify (AK), Togiak (AK), and Willowriver (AK) soils.
Army soils lack a fragmental layer, do not occur on mountains, and have a high elevation of 80 m (262 ft).
Chenega soils are somewhat excessively drained.
Hollow soils are somewhat poorly drained.
Ironcreek lacks a fragmental horizon, has a mean annual precipitation ranging from 300 to 800 mm (12 to 31 in), and has a mean annual air temperature ranging from -5 to -1 degrees C. (23 to 30 degrees F).
Kidazqeni soils have acidic pH values in all horizons.
Malmesbury soils lack a fragmental horizon, do not occur on mountains and have a high elevation of 300 m (984 ft).
Shovelcreek soils have a mean annual precipitation of 450 mm (18 in), a mean annual air temperature of -3 degrees C. (37 degrees F), and frost free period of 70 to 90 days.
Stratify soils have very gravelly sand stratified with thin lenses of silt loam and fine sandy loam in the substratum, substantial mica content in the profile, are moderately well drained, and occur on flood plains and low terraces.
Togiak soils have a mean annual precipitation of about 760 mm (30 in), are somewhat excessively drained, and occur on beach berms of shore complexes. 
Willowriver soils have a buried cambic horizon at depths between 25 to 93 cm (10 to 37 in).
