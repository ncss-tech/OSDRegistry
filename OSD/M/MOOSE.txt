MOOSE SERIES

Depth class: moderately deep to paralithic bedrock 
Drainage class: well drained 
Parent material: volcanic ash influenced coarse-loamy colluvium over residuum weathered from metasedimentary rock 
Landscape: hills 
Landform: hillslopes 
Slope: 20 to 80 percent 
Mean annual precipitation: about 1870 mm (74 in) 
Mean annual air temperature: about 3 degrees C. (37 degrees F.) 
Frost-free period: 130 to 155 days 

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Medial, amorphic Andic Humicryods

TYPICAL PEDON: Moose highly organic medial silt loam in a Sitka spruce community on a west facing convex, linear backslope on a slope of 33 percent at an elevation of 245 m (804 ft) 

     Oi--0 to 8 cm (0 to 3 in); slightly decomposed plant material, dark brown (7.5YR 3/2) moist; brown (7.5YR 5/2) dry; very strongly acid (pH 4.6); clear smooth boundary.

     A--8 to 15 cm (3 to 6 in); highly organic medial silt loam, very dark brown (10YR 2/2) moist, dark grayish brown(10YR 4/2) dry; weak fine granular structure; very friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common medium, fine, and coarse roots throughout; 8 percent gravel by volume; very strongly acid (pH 4.9); clear wavy boundary. 

     AE--15 to 27 cm (6 to 11 in); flaggy silt loam, 50 percent brown (10YR 4/3) moist, pale brown (10YR6/3) dry and 30 percent very dark bluish gray (10B 3/1) moist, bluish gray (10B 5/1) dry and 20 percent very dark gray (10YR 3/1) moist, gray (10YR 5/1) dry; weak fine subangular blocky structure; friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common medium, fine, and coarse roots throughout; 8 percent gravel by volume, 15 percent angular channers by volume; strongly acid (pH 5.3); abrupt wavy boundary. 

     Bhs--27 to 38 cm (11 to 15 in); highly organic medial silt loam, black (5YR 2.5/1) moist, dark gray (5YR 4/1) dry; moderate medium granular structure; friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine, fine, medium, and coarse roots throughout; 4 percent gravel by volume, 5 percent angular channers by volume; strongly acid (pH 5.1); clear smooth boundary. 

     Bs--38 to 46 cm (15 to 18 in); medial silt loam, dark brown (7.5YR 3/2) moist, brown (7.5YR 5/2) dry; moderate fine granular structure; friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common medium, fine and coarse roots throughout; 6 percent gravel by volume; strongly acid (pH 5.4); clear smooth boundary.

     BC--46 to 68 cm (18 to 27 in); medial sandy loam, dark yellowish brown (10YR 4/4) moist, light yellowish brown (10YR 6/4) dry; weak fine subangular blocky structure; very friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common medium and coarse roots throughout; 3 percent by volume, 5 percent by volume;  moderately acid (pH 5.7); clear smooth boundary. 

     2Cr--68 to 93 cm (27 to 37 in); bedrock, yellowish brown (10YR 5/4) moist; strong coherent metasedimentary bedrock. 

TYPE LOCATION: Kenai Peninsula Borough, Alaska; latitude 60.2401900, longitude -149.3559201, datum WGS84.

COMPETING SERIES: These are the Hartnit (WA), Isella (WA), Portofshikof (AK), Sitka (AK), and Wallface (NY) series. 
Hartnit soils lack a moderately deep paralithic contact, have a moderately deep andesite lithic contact, and have a mean annual soil temperature of 5.5 to 6.5 degrees C (42 to 44 degrees F).
Isella soils lack a paralithic contact and have a mean annual soil temperature ranging from 3.5 to 5 degrees C (38 to 41 degrees F).
Portofshikof soils lack a paralithic contact, have buried soil horizons, and have a mean annual soil temperature ranging from 3.5 to 5.5 degrees C (38 to 42 degrees F).
Sitka soils lack a paralithic contact, have buried soil horizons, and have a mean annual soil temperature ranging from 3.5 to 5.5 degrees C (38 to 42 degrees F).
Wallface soils lack a moderately deep paralithic contact, have a moderately deep anorthositic gneiss lithic contact, and have surface fragments consisting of stones and boulders.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: These are the Porcupine, Hive, and Andacher soils. 
Porcupine soils have a shallow lithic contact, loamy skeletal particle size class, and are found on similar landforms on hillslopes. 
Hive soils have organic soil horizons throughout, have aquic conditions throughout, lack andic soil properties, lack a spodic horizon, and are found in depressions on hillslopes. 
Andacher soils have a deep lithic contact, have a skeletal particle size class, and are found on similar landforms on hillslopes.
