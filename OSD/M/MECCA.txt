MECCA SERIES

The Mecca series consists of very deep, well drained soils that formed in alluvium derived from quartz diorite and other igneous sources. Mecca soils are on stream terraces and alluvial fans and have slopes of 2 to 8 percent. The mean annual precipitation is about 250 mm, and the mean annual air temperature is about 17 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Coarse-loamy, mixed, superactive, calcareous, thermic Typic Torrifluvents

TYPICAL PEDON: Mecca soils in an area of Mecca sandy loam on a slope of 5 percent facing east at an elevation of 730 meters under a cover of annual grasses and forbs. (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted). 

  A--0 to 12 cm; brown (10YR 4/3) sandy loam, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2), moist; weak fine subangular blocky structure; very friable, soft, slightly sticky, nonplastic; common fine roots and common very fine roots; common fine dendritic tubular pores; 10 percent gravel; very slight effervescence; neutral (pH 6.6); clear wavy boundary. (3 to 25 cm thick)

  C--12 to 75 cm; brown (10YR 4/3) sandy loam, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2), moist; structureless massive structure; very friable, soft, slightly sticky, nonplastic; common very fine roots; common fine irregular pores; 13 percent gravel; slight effervescence; slightly alkaline (pH 7.5); clear wavy boundary. (10 to 125 cm thick)

  Ck1--75 to 100 cm; brown (10YR 4/3) gravelly loamy coarse sand, dark brown (10YR 3/3), moist; structureless single grain structure; loose, loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; 2 percent irregular carbonate masses in matrix; 20 percent gravel; violent effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.1); clear smooth boundary. (10 to 25 cm thick)

  Ck2--100 to 150 cm; brown (10YR 4/3) gravelly coarse sandy loam, dark brown (10YR 3/3), moist; structureless massive structure; very friable, soft, slightly sticky, nonplastic; 3 percent threadlike carbonate masses in matrix; 20 percent gravel; violent effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.4) (10 to 50 cm thick)

TYPE LOCATION: San Diego County, California; approximately 4 km (2.5 miles) east of the intersection of the Great Southern Overland Stage Route and Great Sandy Trail. USGS 7.5 minute topographic quadrangle: Earthquake Valley, California. WGS84 33.0735400 degrees latitude, -116.4199700 degrees longitude; UTM Zone 11, 3659589 meters N, 554139 meters E NAD83. 

RANGE IN CHARACTERISTICS:
Mean annual soil temperature: 18 degrees C.
Soil moisture: Usually dry in all parts from late March or early April until late November or early December, and usually moist in some parts the rest of the year. Typic Aridic soil moisture regime. 

Rock fragments: 0 to 25 percent gravel throughout
Reaction: neutral to moderately alkaline.
Clay content: 5 to 15 percent by average in the particle-size control section.

COMPETING SERIES:
These are the Anthony (AZ), Excelsior (CA; MLRA 17), Gila (AZ), Grabe (AZ), Ireteba (NV), Junction (UT), Rucker (AZ), Tobler (UT), and Victorville (CA) series. Anthony, Excelsior, Gila, Grabe, Ireteba,  Rucker, and Victorville soils formed in stratified alluvium that may contain textures finer than sandy loam. Junction and Tobler soils have hues redder than 10YR. Excelsior soils have mean annual precipitation less than 200 millimeters (8 inches). Victorville soils are moderately well drained.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Cajon, Indio, Morongo, Pinecity, Pioneertown, and Ramona soils:
Cajon soils: greater than 80 percent sand throughout the particle control section
Indio soils: have a hyperthermic soil moisture regime
Morongo soils: greater than 80 percent sand throughout the particle control section
Pinecity soils: 25 to 50 centimeters to a paralithic contact of igneous rock 
Pioneertown soils: 25 to 50 centimeters to a lithic contact of igneous rock
Ramona soils: greater than 18 percent clay in the particle control section

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: Well drained; moderately rapid to moderate saturated hydraulic conductivity.

USE AND VEGETATION: Used for wildlife and recreation. Native vegetation is creosote, California juniper, Mojave yucca, Gander's cholla, big galleta, and annual grasses.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: Sonoran Basin and Range, MLRA 40. This soil is moderately extensive.
