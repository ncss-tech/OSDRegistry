NORTHWATER SERIES

The Northwater series consists of deep, well drained soils formed in colluvium over residuum from sedimentary and metasedimentary rocks. These soils are on mountain slopes, ridges, and valley floors. Slopes are 6 to 80 percent. Mean annual precipitation is about 575 mm, and mean annual temperature is about 4 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Loamy-skeletal, mixed, superactive Pachic Palecryolls

TYPICAL PEDON: Northwater loam. (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted.)

 Oe--0 to 5 cm; moderately decomposed plant material.

 A1--5 to 30 cm; brown (10YR 4/3) loam, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) moist; moderate fine granular structure; soft, very friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many fine and very fine roots; neutral; clear smooth boundary.

 A2--30 to 69 cm; dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2) loam, very dark brown (10YR 2/2) moist; moderate fine granular structure; slightly hard, very friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many fine and very fine roots; 10 percent gravel; neutral; clear wavy boundary.

 Bt--69 to 132 cm; light brown (7.5YR 6/4) very channery clay loam, brown (7.5YR 4/4) moist; moderate medium subangular blocky structure parting to moderate fine subangular blocky; hard, friable, sticky and plastic; common medium and coarse roots; common clay films and organic coats on faces of peds and on surfaces along pores; 50 percent channers; neutral; clear wavy boundary.

 R--132 cm; fractured sandstone.

TYPE LOCATION: Garfield County, Colorado; 400 feet south and 150 feet west of the center of Sec. 12, T. 5 S., R. 96 W.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: None listed.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: Well drained, 1.5 to 15.0 um/sec

USE AND VEGETATION: Northwater soils are primarily used for recreation, wildlife habitat, and limited livestock grazing. The potential native vegetation is snowberry, Columbia needlegrass, nodding brome, elk sedge, slender wheatgrass and Thurber's fescue.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: Northwater soils are of moderate extent in Colorado and southeastern Idaho; MLRAs 13, 47, and 48A.

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: Bozeman, Montana

SERIES ESTABLISHED: Paonia Area, Colorado, 1976.

REMARKS: Diagnostic horizons and features recognized in this pedon:
Mollic epipedon--0 to 69 cm (A1 and A2 horizons)
Argillic horizon--69 to 132 cm (Bt horizon)
Lithic contact--132 cm (R horizon)

Particle-size control section--69 to 119 cm

Northwater soils have a cryic temperature regime and an udic moisture regime.
