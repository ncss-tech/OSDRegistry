BIGHORN SERIES

The Bighorn series consists of very shallow to shallow, excessively drained soils that are formed in residuum derived from gneiss, granite, and tonalite. Bighorn soils are on mountains with slopes 15 to 60 percent. The mean annual precipitation is 155 mm and the mean annual air temperature is about 22 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Mixed, hyperthermic, shallow Typic Torripsamments

TYPICAL PEDON: Bighorn gravelly loamy coarse sand on slope of 16 percent at an elevation of 392 meters under a cover of brittlebush, wolf's opuntia, creosote, and ocotillo. Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted. Surface fragment cover is 75 percent gravel, 5 percent cobbles, 5 percent stones, and 1 percent boulders.

  A--0 to 7 cm; gravelly loamy coarse sand, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) moist; weak fine granular structure; very friable, soft, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine and fine roots; common fine interstitial pores; moderately alkaline (pH 8.0); noneffervescent; abrupt wavy boundary. (5 to 24 cm thick)

  Cr--7 to 32 cm; weathered tonalite bedrock.

TYPE LOCATION: San Diego County, California; approximately 2.9 kilometers (1.9 miles) south and 1.5 kilometers (0.9 miles) west of the intersection of Tubb Canyon Rd and Borrego Springs Road; 33.18684 degrees latitude, -116.38509 degrees longitude WGS84; UTM Zone 11 3672168 N, 557321 E. U.S.G.S Quad Name: Tubb Canyon, California.

USE AND VEGETATION: This soil is used for recreation and wildlife habitat. Vegetation is dominantly brittlebush, wolf's opuntia, creosote, ocotillo, and stork's bill.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: The soils are of small extent and are mapped in the lower Colorado Desert of California. MLRA: 40
