BRICKER SERIES

The Bricker series consists of very deep, well drained soils formed in alluvium and colluvium from mixed metasedimentary rock.  These soils occur on alluvial fans, stream terraces, escarpments, hills and mountain slopes.  Slopes are 0 to 45 percent.  Mean annual precipitation is about 435 mm and mean annual air temperature is about 5.0 degrees C. 

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Loamy-skeletal, mixed, superactive, frigid Typic Haploxerepts

TYPICAL PEDON:  Bricker loam, in coniferous forest. This profile was described on an 40 percent slope at 1,220 meters elevation.  (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted.)

    Oi--0 to 4 centimeters; needles, twigs, decomposing organic materials; very strongly acid (pH 5.0).

    A--4 to 14 cm; very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) sandy loam, black (10YR 2/1) moist; weak fine granular structure; slightly hard, friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine and common fine roots; common very fine irregular pores; slightly acid (pH 6.2); clear smooth boundary. 

    Bw--14 to 45 cm; yellowish brown (10YR 5/4) extremely gravelly loam, brown (10YR 4/3) moist; weak fine subangular blocky structure; slightly hard, friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine, common fine and few medium roots; many very fine and few fine irregular pores; slightly acid (pH 6.4); clear wavy boundary. 

    BC--45 to 69 cm; yellowish brown (10YR 5/4) extremely gravelly loam, dark yellowish brown (10YR 4/4) moist; weak fine subangular blocky structure; slightly hard, friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine, common fine and few medium roots; many very fine and few fine irregular pores; slightly acid (pH 6.4); clear wavy boundary.

    C--69 to 152 cm; light brown (7.5YR 6/4) extremely gravelly sandy loam, brown (7.5YR 5/4) moist; massive; loose, nonsticky and nonplastic; few very fine roots; common very fine interstitial pores; neutral (pH 6.6).

TYPE LOCATION:  Mineral County, Montana; approximately 783 meters north and 302 meters east of the southeast corner of Sec. 16, T. 15 N., R. 25 W.; Stark North topographic quadrangle; latitude 47.1604750, longitude -114.4862780, datum WGS 84.

COMPETING SERIES:
Farva--is moderately deep to paralithic contact.
Lostbasin--is moderately deep to lithic contact.
Segundo--is deep and derived from colluvial rhyolite and andesite parent materials.

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING:
Landform - stream terraces, escarpments, alluvial fans, hills and mountain slopes. 
Elevation - 550 to 1100 meters. 
Slope - 0 to 45 percent. 
Parent material - alluvium and colluvium derived from metasedimentary rock. 
Climate - long, cold winters; moist springs; short warm summers. 
Mean annual precipitation - 307 to 486 mm.
Mean annual air temperature - 4.0 to 7.0 degrees C.  
Frost-free period - 70 to 120 days. 

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: Bricker soils are associated with Jazzbo, Tamely and Yellowbay soils. 

DRAINAGE AND PERMEABILITY:  Well drained; moderate permeability (1 to 10 um/s). 

USE AND VEGETATION:  Bricker soils are used mainly for recreation, timber production, and wildlife habitat.  The vegetation is ponderosa pine and Douglas-fir with bluebunch wheatgrass, arrowleaf balsamroot, lupine, Oregon grape, pine grass, and kinnikinnick in the understory. 

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT:  Bricker soils are of small extent in northwestern Montana. MLRA 43A and 44A.

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE:  Bozeman, Montana.

SERIES PROPOSED:  Lolo National Forest area in Mineral County, Montana, 2026. 

REMARKS:  Diagnostic horizons and features recognized in this pedon are:
Ochric epipedon - from 0 to 10 cm (A horizon)
Cambic horizon - from 14 to 45 cm (Bw horizon)

Bricker soils have a frigid temperature regime and a xeric moisture regime.
