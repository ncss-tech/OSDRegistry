YENT SERIES

Drainage class--Very poorly drained

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Loamy, mixed, euic, hyperthermic Terric Haplosaprists

TYPICAL PEDON: Yent woody muck in a freshwater marsh 0.9 meter above sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Oase--0 to 59 centimeters; black (2.5Y 2.5/1) broken face and very dark brown (10YR 2/2) rubbed woody muck; 20 percent fibers, 15 percent rubbed; von Post H7; many very fine and fine roots; 25 percent wood fragments; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.6 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.2), strongly acid (pH 5.1) oxidized pH; gradual boundary (40 to 120 centimeters thick)

 Aseg--59 to 88 centimeters; black (10YR 2/1) mucky loam; massive; moderately sticky, moderately plastic; moderately fluid; many very fine and common fine roots; 10 percent wood fragments; strong sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.4 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.4), very strongly acid (pH 4.5) oxidized pH; clear boundary (25 to 80 centimeters thick)

 Cseg--88 to 200 centimeters; 60 percent black (N 2.5/), 20 percent very dark greenish gray (10Y 3/1), and 20 percent dark greenish gray (10Y 4/1) clay loam; massive; moderately sticky, moderately plastic; moderately fluid; few fine roots at the top of the horizon; strong sulfurous odor; color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; strong effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; very slightly saline (2.8 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.3), strongly acid (pH 5.3) oxidized pH

COMPETING SERIES:
These are the Delcomb and Hacokewa soils
Delcomb soils--formed in herbaceous organic material over silty sediments on brackish coastal marshes, do not have sulfurous odor and are slightly finer textured in the substratum layers.
Hacokewa soils--formed in herbaceous organic material over loamy lagoonal deposits in polyhaline barrier islands and tidal marshes.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Blackrail, LaFitte, Maurepas, Opilwa, and Romalea soils.
Blackrail soils--formed in sandy alluvium and do not have organic soil materials thicker than 40 centimeters beginning at the soil surface.
LaFitte soils--have greater than 130 centimeters of herbaceous organic materials beginning at the soil surface and occur in oligohaline and mesohaline tidal marshes.
Maurepas soils--formed in woody plant remains in large backswamps of coastal areas and have greater than 130 centimeters of organic soil materials beginning at the soil surface.
Opilwa soils--formed in fine-loamy alluvial deposits over woody organic material and occur on broad floodplains and swamps.
Romalea soils--formed in fine-loamy alluvial and estuarine deposits and occur in freshwater and oligohaline marshes, and along tidal river floodplains. 

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Very low to moderately low
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Very frequent and frequent, for brief duration. Yent soils are flooded by wind and lunar tides, and occasional overbank flow. Inundation depths on Yent soils are irregular. 

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Yent soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Yent soils occurring in freshwater marshes are singularly dominated by sawgrass (Cladium jamaicense). In local highs, red maple (Acer rubrum) or bald cypress (Taxodium distichum) saplings may be observed. In oligohaline marshes, sawgrass is more interspersed amongst more dominant stands of black needlerush (Juncus roemerianus).

REMARKS:
The series is named after Yent Bayou. Yent soils were previously mapped as the Bohicket, Brickyard, Chowan, Kenner, Maurepas, and Tisonia soil series in Florida soil surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Sapric soil materials--The zone from 0 to 59 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is less than one-sixth of the original soil volume.
Hyposulfidic materials--The zone from 0 to 200 centimeters has an initial pH that decreases by 1 or more units but remains at a value greater than 4.0 after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Fluidity--The zone from 59 to 200 centimeters has a fluidity class of moderately fluid.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
