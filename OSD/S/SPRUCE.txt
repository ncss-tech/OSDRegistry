SPRUCE SERIES

Depth class: very deep
Drainage class: well drained to somewhat poorly drained
Parent material: coarse-loamy alluvium over sandy and gravelly alluvium derived from metasedimentary rock
Landscape: bajadas, fan piedmonts, valleys
Landform: flood plains, alluvial fans
Slope: 0 to 6 percent
Mean annual precipitation: about 1870 mm (73 in)
Mean annual air temperature: about 3 degrees C (37 F)
Frost-free period: 130 to 155 days

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Coarse-loamy over sandy or sandy-skeletal, mixed, superactive, nonacid Typic Cryofluvents

TYPICAL PEDON: Spruce silt loam in a maritime forest community on a north facing high flood plain on a slope of 1 percent at an elevation of 60m (197 ft).

     Oe--0 to 2 cm (0 to 1 in); moderately decomposed plant material, very dark brown (7.5YR 2.5/2) moist, brown (7.5YR 4/2) dry; strongly acid, (pH 5.3), abrupt smooth boundary.

     A--2 to 4 cm (1 to 2 in); silt loam, black (7.5YR 2.5/1) moist, dark gray (7.5YR 4/1) dry; moderate medium granular structure; very friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common medium and coarse roots and many fine roots; moderately acid (pH 6.0); abrupt smooth boundary.

     C1--4 to 65 cm (2 to 26 in); silt loam, very dark gray (2.5Y 3/1) moist, gray (2.5Y 5/1) dry; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable, nonsticky, nonplastic; common medium and coarse roots and many fine roots; moderately acid (pH 6.0); clear smooth boundary.

     C2--65 to 200 cm (26 to 79 in); very gravelly sand, light olive brown (2.5Y 5/3) moist, variegated pale yellow (2.5Y 7/3) dry; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; common fine roots; 55 percent rounded gravels by volume, slightly acid (pH 6.3).

TYPE LOCATION: Kenai Peninsula Borough, Alaska: latitude 60.1608333, longitude -149.4025000, datum WGS84

Some pedons have stratified textures.

COMPETING SERIES: These are the Jarvis (AK), Klute (AK), Klutina (AK), Niklason (AK), Portdick (AK), and Tonowek (AK) series.
Jarvis soils have a mean annual precipitation of 254 to 381 mm (10 to 15 in).
Klute soils have a mean annual precipitation of 406 mm (16 in) and mean annual air temperature of 660 mm (26 degrees F).
Klutina soils have a mean annual precipitation of 305 mm (12 in) and mean annual air temperature of -5 to -3 degrees C (23 to 27 degrees F).
Niklason soils have a mean annual precipitation of 36 to76 cm (14 to 30 in).
Portdick soils are moderately well drained and somewhat poorly drained.
Tonowek soils have a mean annual precipitation of 254 mm (100 in).

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING:
Elevation: 0 to 155 meters (0 to 509 feet)
Parent material: coarse-loamy alluvium over sandy and gravelly alluvium derived from metasedimentary rock
Landform: flood plains or alluvial fans
Slopes: 0 to 5 percent
Mean annual precipitation: 1730 to 2020 mm (68 to 80 in)
Mean annual air temperature: 2 to 4 degrees C. (36 to 39 degrees F)
Frost free period: 130 to 155 days

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: These are the Army, Iron, and Sandspit soils.
Army soils have a sandy-skeletal particle size class and occur on similar landforms on floodplains and alluvial fans.
Iron soils are orthents and have sandy-skeletal particle size class and occur on similar landforms on flood plain positions.
Sandspit soils are poorly drained and have a sandy-skeletal particle size class and occur on similar landforms on flood plains.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class: well drained
Saturated Hydraulic Conductivity: very high for the O and C2 horizons, moderately high to high for the A, and C1 horizons
Permeability: rapid for the O and C2 horizons, moderate to rapid for the A and C1 horizons
Runoff: low
Flooding: rarely to occasional, brief

USE AND VEGETATION:
Use: wildlife habitat, recreation, urban land, and subsistence.
Native vegetation: rarely flooded soils have Sitka alder, bride's feathers, dicranum moss, spreading woodfern, splendid feather moss, clubmoss, sidebells wintergreen, Sitka spruce, Schreber's big red stem moss, balsam poplar, wintergreen, rhizomnium moss, salmonberry, claspleaf twistedstalk, western hemlock, oval-leaf blueberry.
Occasionally flooded phases: red baneberry, Sitka alder, narcissus anemone, bride's feathers, aster, northern groundcone, field horsetail, variegated scouringrush, fragrant bedstraw, largeleaf avens, common cowparsnip, devilsclub, sweetcicely, sweetroot, Sitka spruce, northern green orchid, Schreber's big red stem moss, balsam poplar, western rattlesnakeroot, wintergreen, buttercup, woodland buttercup, goose neck moss, blackberry, salmonberry, Menzies' burnet, Sitka willow, claspleaf twistedstalk, squashberry, violet.
