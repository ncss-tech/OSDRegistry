STRINGAM SERIES

The Stringam series consists of very deep, well drained soils that formed in colluvium, slope alluvium, or eolian deposits derived from rhyolite, obsidian, chert, or sedimentary rock; some with calcareous influence. These soils are on mountain slopes, hillslopes, fan remnants, lava plains, interfluves and swales. Slopes are 1 to 65 [KN-NIFI1.1][CR1.2]percent. Mean annual precipitation is about 625 mm and mean annual air temperature is about 2.2 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Fine-loamy, mixed, superactive Mollic Haplocryalfs

TYPICAL PEDON:  Stringam loam in forestland. (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise stated.)

     Oi--0 to 2 cm; slightly decomposed plant material ( needles and twigs).

     A1--2 to 10 cm: grayish brown (10YR 5/2) loam, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) moist; weak fine subangular blocky structure; soft, very friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine and fine roots; few very fine tubular pores; moderately acid (pH 5.8); clear irregular boundary.

     A2--10 to 20 cm; brown (10YR 5/3) sandy loam, dark yellowish brown (10YR 3/4) moist; moderate fine subangular blocky structure; soft, very friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine and fine roots; few very fine tubular pores; moderately acid (pH 5.8); clear wavy boundary.

     AB--20 to 30 cm; pale brown (10YR 6/3) sandy loam, dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2) moist; moderate medium and fine subangular blocky structure; soft, very friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine and fine roots; few very fine tubular pores; moderately acid (pH 5.8); clear wavy boundary.

     Bt1--30 to 56 cm; pale brown (10YR 6/3) sandy clay loam; brown (10YR 4/3) moist; moderate medium prismatic structure parting to moderate medium and fine subangular blocky; slightly hard, friable, slightly sticky, and plastic; few very fine and fine roots; few very fine tubular pores; few thin clay films on faces of peds; 5 percent gravels; moderately acid (pH 5.9); clear wavy boundary.

     Bt2--56 to 86 cm; pale brown (10YR 6/3) sandy clay loam, brown (10YR 4/3) moist; moderate fine subangular block structure; hard, very firm, slightly sticky and plastic; few very fine and fine roots; few very fine tubular pores; few thin clay films on faces of peds and very few thin clay films bridging mineral grains; 5 percent gravels; moderately acid (pH 5.9); clear wavy boundary.

     Bt3--86 to 142 cm; light brownish gray (10YR 6/2) sandy clay loam, brown (10YR 5/3) moist; moderate fine subangular blocky structure; hard, firm, slightly sticky and plastic; few thin clay films on faces of peds and very few thin clay films bridging mineral grains; 10 percent gravels; moderately acid (pH 5.8); clear wavy boundary.

     C1--142 to 155 cm; light brownish gray (10YR 6/2) sandy clay loam, brown (10YR 5/3) moist; massive; hard, very firm, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; 10 percent gravels; slightly acid (pH 6.1).

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: Well drained, 0.5 to 100.0 um/sec.

USE AND VEGETATION:  Stringam soils are primarily used for timber production and grazing. Potential native vegetation is douglas-fir, pine reedgrass, Idaho fescue, Kentucky bluegrass, and arrowleaf balsamroot.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: Stingam soils are of moderate extent in southeast Idaho and western Wyoming; MLRAs 13, 43B.

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: Bozeman, Montana
