VERTINE SERIES

The Vertine series consists of moderately deep, somewhat poorly drained soils that formed in residuum weathered from travertine. These soils are on travertine terraces. Slopes are 0 to 6 percent. Mean annual precipitation is about 450 millimeters, and mean annual temperature is about 3 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Loamy-skeletal, carbonatic, frigid Oxyaquic Haploxerolls

TYPICAL PEDON: Vertine sandy loam in a meadow. (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted.)

 Oi--0 to 5 cm; slightly decomposed plant material and live roots; dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2), very dark brown (10YR 2/2) moist; abrupt smooth boundary.

 A--5 to 24 cm; grayish brown (10YR 5/2) sandy loam, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) moist; moderate medium subangular blocky structure parting to strong very fine and fine granular; soft, very friable, slightly sticky and slightly plastic; many very fine and few fine roots; many very fine irregular pores; many fine irregular soft secondary calcium carbonate masses and seams; 80 percent calcium carbonate equivalent; violently effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.7); abrupt wavy boundary.

 C1--24 to 28 cm; very pale brown (10YR 8/3) extremely channery loam, very pale brown (10YR 7/3) moist; common fine and medium prominent strong brown (7.5YR 5/6) mottles; strong very thick platy structure; extremely hard, extremely firm, nonsticky and nonplastic; common very fine and few fine roots; many very fine, fine and medium irregular and tubular pores; 90 percent calcium carbonate equivalent; violently effervescent; 65 percent travertine channers; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); abrupt wavy boundary.

 C2--28 to 41 cm; very pale brown (10YR 8/2) gravelly loamy sand, pale brown (10YR 6/3) moist; common fine, medium and coarse prominent reddish yellow (7.5YR 6/6) mottles; strong fine subangular blocky structure; slightly hard, very friable, nonsticky and nonplastic; many very fine and common fine roots; common very fine tubular and many very fine irregular pores; 90 percent calcium carbonate equivalent; violently effervescent; 20 percent travertine gravel; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8). abrupt wavy boundary.

 C3--41 to 61 cm; very pale brown (10YR 8/2) extremely channery loam, very pale brown (10YR 7/4) moist; few fine prominent reddish yellow (7.5YR 6/6) mottles; strong very thick platy structure; extremely hard, extremely firm, nonsticky and nonplastic; few very fine and fine roots; many very fine, fine and medium irregular and tubular pores; 85 percent calcium carbonate equivalent; violently effervescent; 80 percent travertine channers; moderately alkaline (pH 7.9); clear wavy boundary.

 C4--61 to 79 cm; light gray (10YR 7/2) gravelly sand, grayish brown (10YR 5/2) moist; common fine and medium prominent reddish yellow (7.5YR 6/6 and 7.5YR 7/6) mottles; single grain; loose; few very fine roots; many very fine irregular pores; violently effervescent (85 percent calcium carbonate equivalent); 20 percent travertine gravel; moderately alkaline (pH 8.0).

 R--79 cm; very strongly cemented travertine.

TYPE LOCATION: Caribou County, Idaho; USGS China Hat topographic quadrangle; latitude 42.80222 degrees north, longitude 111.53527 degrees west, datum WGS84.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: None listed.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: Somewhat poorly drained, 1.5 to 50.0 um/sec in the upper part, 150.0 to 700.0 um/sec in the lower part.

USE AND VEGETATION: Vertine soils are primarily used for nonirrigated pasture. The potential native vegetation is shrubby cinquefoil, bluegrass and sedges.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: Vertine soils are of small extent in southeastern Idaho; MLRA 13.

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: Bozeman, Montana

SERIES PROPOSED: Caribou County, Idaho, 1993. The name is coined.

REMARKS: Diagnostic horizons and features recognized in this pedon:
Mottles-- result of minerals deposited by geologic action rather than redoximorphic features.
Mollic epipedon--5 to 24 cm (A horizon)
Lithic contact--79 cm (R horizon)

Particle-size control section--30 to 79 cm

Vertine soils have a frigid temperature regime and a xeric moisture regime.
