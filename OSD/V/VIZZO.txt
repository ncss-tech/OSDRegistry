VIZZO SERIES

The Vizzo series consists of very deep, excessively drained soils that formed in alluvium from basalt and volcanic rock. Vizzo soils are on alluvial fans, fan piedmonts, and washes with linear east facing slopes of 0 to 3 percent. The mean annual precipitation is about 117 mm, and the mean annual temperature is about 21 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Sandy-skeletal, mixed, hyperthermic Vitrandic Torriorthents

TYPICAL PEDON: Vizzo gravelly coarse sand on a slope of 2 percent under a cover of burrobush, brittlebush, creosote, cryptantha, brome, silver cholla, agave, teddy bear cholla at an elevation of 298 meters. Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted. Surface fragment cover is 60 percent gravel, 10 percent cobbles, and 1 percent stones.

  A--0 to 5 cm; pinkish gray (7.5YR 6/2) gravelly coarse sand, brown (7.5YR 4/4) moist; weak thin platy structure; very friable, soft, nonsticky, nonplastic; few finely disseminated carbonates throughout; common fine vesicular pores; 15 percent gravel; slight effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.1); abrupt smooth boundary. (4 to 8 cm thick)

  C1--5 to 28 cm; pinkish gray (7.5YR 6/2) stratified gravelly coarse sand and very gravelly coarse sand, brown (7.5YR 5/3) moist; massive; very friable, soft, nonsticky, nonplastic; few finely disseminated carbonates throughout; few distinct silica masses on bottom of rock fragments; 50 percent gravel; slight effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.3); clear wavy boundary. (23 to 100 cm thick) 

  C2--28 to 70 cm; pinkish gray (7.5YR 6/2) gravelly coarse sand, brown (7.5YR 5/4) moist; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; common finely disseminated carbonates throughout; common fine fain carbonate masses on bottom of rock fragments; 30 percent gravel; slight effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.4); clear wavy boundary. (33 to 130 cm thick) 

  C3--70 to 150 cm; pinkish gray (7.5YR 6/2) extremely gravelly coarse sand, brown (7.5YR 5/4) moist; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; common finely disseminated carbonates throughout; few faint carbonate masses on bottom of rock fragments; common distinct silica masses on bottom of rock fragments; 65 percent gravel, strong effervescence; moderately alkaline (pH 8.4). (15 to 47 cm thick) 

TYPE LOCATION:  San Diego County, California; about 790 feet north of the intersection of Sweeney Pass Road and Mortero Canyon Road, Northeast portion of sec. 12, T. 16 S, R. 08 E. USGS 7.5 minute topographic quadrangle: Carrizo Mountain, California. WGS84 32.7952000 latitude and -116.1078200 longitude; UTM Zone 11, 3628935 meters N and 583537 meters E WGS84. 

RANGE IN CHARACTERISTICS:
Soil Temperature: Hyperthermic temperature regime. Mean annual soil temperature at 50 cm is 23 degrees C, and the difference between mean summer and mean winter soil temperature is greater than 6 degrees C.

Soil Moisture: Typic-aridic moisture regime. Usually dry, moist in some parts for short periods during winter and early spring and for 10 to 20 days cumulative between July and September following summer convection storms.

COMPETING SERIES:
There are no other series in this family. Similar soils in other families include Carrizo and Rizzo.
Carrizo: are formed in mixed igneous alluvium and do not have visible secondary carbonates. 

Rizzo: have mean annual soil temperatures that average greater than 25 degrees C, do not receive appreciable summer precipitation, and are generally dry throughout the moisture control section for most of the year.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: These are the Buzzardsprings, Cajon, Carsitas, Emptygun, Goldrose, Lostpalms, and Rizzo soils. Buzzardsprings and Emptygun are found on nearby fan remnants and have a calcic horizon. Cajon, Carsitas, Goldrose, and Rizzo soils occur on similar landscapes. Cajon, Carsitas, and Goldrose have on average fewer than 35% rock fragments in the particle size control section. Lostpalms soils are on hills and pediments and are shallow to lithic contact.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: Excessively drained; high to very high saturated hydraulic conductivity. 

USE AND VEGETATION: This soil is used for recreation and wildlife habitat. Vegetation is dominantly brittlebush, cryptantha, creosote, burrobush, ocotillo, agave, brome, silver cholla, and teddybear cholla.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: The soils are of small extent and are mapped in the lower Colorado Desert of California. MLRA: 40  

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: Davis, California

SERIES ESTABLISHED: San Diego County, California, Anza-Borrego Area Soil Survey, 2026. The soil is named after the volcanic parent material and soil series that occur in similar settings (Carrizo and Rizzo).
