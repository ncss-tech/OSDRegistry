TAXONOMIC CLASS: Fine-silty, spolic, mixed, active, mesic Aquic Eutrudepts

TYPICAL PEDON: Tosa silt loam, on a 1 percent slope in a park lawn at an elevation of 223 meters above mean sea level. (Colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated.)

 ^A--0 to 23 centimeters (0 to 9 inches); very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2), grayish brown (10YR 5/2), dry; silt loam; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable; slightly effervescent; neutral (pH 7.0); clear smooth boundary (8 to 30 centimeters thick)

 ^Bw--23 to 76 centimeters (9 to 30 inches); brown (10YR 5/3) silty clay loam; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable; 3 percent fine faint dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2) iron depletions in matrix and 8 percent fine distinct yellowish brown (10YR 5/6) iron-manganese masses in matrix; 5 percent gravel; strongly effervescent; moderately alkaline (pH 7.9); gradual wavy boundary (20 to 55 centimeters thick)

 ^Bg--76 to 122 centimeters (30 to 48 inches); grayish brown (10YR 5/2) silty clay loam; moderate medium subangular structure; friable; 8 percent fine prominent yellowish brown (10YR 5/6) iron-manganese masses in matrix; 5 percent gravel; slightly effervescent; moderately alkaline (pH 7.9); gradual wavy boundary (0 to 30 centimeters thick)

 ^Cg--122 to 200 centimeters (48 to 79 inches); 60 percent grayish brown (10YR 5/2) and 40 percent brown (10YR 5/3) stratified silt loam and silty clay loam; massive; firm; 8 percent fine prominent yellowish brown (10YR 5/6) iron-manganese masses in matrix; slightly effervescent; moderately alkaline (pH 8.0)

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: These are the Milwaukee and Thiensville series which also formed in human-transported material and occur on anthropogenic landforms.

Milwaukee soils are deeper than 100 cm to aquic conditions and occur on fills on nearby ground moraines.
Thiensville soils have aquic conditions at the surface, occur on leveled land in nearby depressions and have occasional, very brief ponding.
