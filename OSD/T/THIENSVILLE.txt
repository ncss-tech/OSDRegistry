TYPICAL PEDON: Thiensville mucky silt loam on a 1 percent slope in an amended and cultivated urban community garden at about 210 meters above mean sea level. (Colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated.)

 ^A--0 to 15 centimeters (0 to 6 inches); black (10YR 2/1) mucky silt loam, dark gray (10YR 4/1) dry; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable; many medium and fine roots throughout; many very fine and fine irregular pores; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); clear smooth boundary

 ^Au--15 to 38 centimeters(6 to 15 inches); 95 percent very dark gray (2.5Y 3/1) and 5 percent olive brown (2.5Y 4/3) very gravelly loam, gray (2.5Y 5/1) dry; moderate fine moderate medium granular structure; very firm from compaction; few very fine and fine roots; few very fine and fine irregular pores; 10 percent medium gravel; 3 percent gravel sized concrete artifacts, 3 percent gravel sized brick artifacts, 3 percent gravel sized plastic artifacts, 10 percent gravel sized coal combustion byproducts; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); clear wavy boundary (Combined thickness of ^A horizons is 18 to 40 centimeters)

 ^Bug--38 to 61 centimeters (15 to 24 inches); 90 percent dark gray (2.5Y 4/1) and 10 percent olive (5Y 4/3) silty clay loam; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable; few medium irregular pores; 25 percent distinct very dark brown (10YR 2/2) organic stains on all faces of peds; 2 percent very fine distinct black (10YR 2/1) iron-manganese nodules in the matrix; 6 percent fine prominent yellowish brown (10YR 5/6) iron-manganese masses in the matrix; 6 percent medium gravel; 3 percent gravel-sized concrete artifacts and 5 percent gravel sized coal combustion byproducts; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); clear wavy boundary

 ^Bg--61 to 79 centimeters (24 to 31 inches); 80 percent dark gray (2.5Y 4/1), 10 percent dark grayish brown (2.5Y 4/2), and 10 percent greenish gray (5GY 6/1) silty clay; moderate medium subangular blocky structure; friable; few medium irregular pores; 15 percent faint very dark gray (10YR 3/1) organic stains on all faces of peds; 2 percent fine distinct black (10YR 2/1) iron-manganese nodules throughout; 6 percent medium prominent light olive brown (2.5Y 5/6) iron-manganese masses in the matrix; 2 percent medium gravel; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); gradual wavy boundary (Combined thickness of the ^B horizons is 20 to 50 centimeters)

 ^Cug1--79 to 86 centimeters (31 to 34 inches); greenish gray (10GY 6/1) silty clay; massive; firm; 6 percent medium prominent light olive brown (2.5Y 5/6) iron-manganese masses in the matrix; 2 percent medium gravel; 2 percent gravel sized coal combustion byproducts; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); clear smooth boundary. (Thickness 6 to 20 centimeters)

 ^Aub--86 to 102 centimeters (35 to 40 inches); 95 percent very dark gray (10YR 3/1) and 5 percent gray (5Y 5/1) silty clay loam; weak fine relict granular structure; friable; 1 percent fine gravel; 2 percent gravel sized coal combustion byproducts; slightly alkaline (pH 7.6); clear wavy boundary (0 to 18 centimeters thick)

 ^C'ug2--102 to 124 centimeters (40 to 49 inches); olive gray (5Y 5/2) silty clay loam; massive; firm; 18 percent medium prominent light olive brown (2.5Y 5/6) iron-manganese masses in the matrix; 3 percent fine gravel; 2 percent medium gravel sized artifacts of brick and 3 percent gravel sized coal combustion byproducts; moderate sulfurous odor; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline (pH 7.8); gradual wavy boundary

 ^C'ug3--124 to 200 centimeters (49 to 79 inches); olive gray (5Y 5/2) silt loam; massive; friable; 18 percent medium prominent light olive brown (2.5Y 5/6) iron-manganese masses in the matrix; 4 percent fine gravel; 5 percent gravel sized coal combustion byproducts; moderate sulfurous odor; slightly effervescent; slightly alkaline, pH 7.8

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Milwaukee and Tosa series which also occur on anthropogenic landforms with over 50 centimeters of human-transported material.

Milwaukee soils are deeper than 100 cm to aquic conditions and occur on fills on nearby hillslopes and summits.
Tosa soils have aquic conditions between 30 and 60 centimeters and occur on fills on nearby terraces and foot slopes.
