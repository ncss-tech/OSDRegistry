TOWER GROVE SERIES

Depth class: Very deep
Drainage class: Moderately well drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity: Moderately high to high 
Parent material: Human-transported materials mostly derived from nearby loess deposits and over Peoria loess
Geomorphic location: Leveled land on hillslopes and interfluves
Slope range: 0 to 30 percent
Mean annual temperature: 12 degrees C
Mean annual precipitation: 1050 millimeters 

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Fine-silty, mixed, superactive, mesic Oxyaquic Hapludalfs

TYPICAL PEDON: Tower Grove silt loam, 2 percent slope in a city park under sod grass, in an area where minor land-leveling has occurred (Colors are for moist soil unless otherwise noted.)

 ^Au--0 to 10 centimeters; dark brown (10YR 3/3) silt loam; weak very fine subangular blocky structure; friable; common very fine and fine roots throughout; common fine interstitial pores; 1 percent gravel-sized brick artifacts; neutral (pH 6.8); clear smooth boundary (7 to 18 cm thick)

 ^Cu--10 to 32 centimeters; 50 percent dark grayish brown (10YR 4/2) and 35 percent brown (10YR 4/3) and 15 percent reddish brown (5YR 4/3) silt loam; massive; friable; few fine roots throughout; few fine interstitial pores; 2 percent gravel-sized brick and 3 percent gravel-sized bitumen (asphalt) artifacts; slightly alkaline (pH 7.6); abrupt wavy boundary (12 to 28 cm thick)

 2Bt1--32 to 43 centimeters; 70 percent dark brown (10YR 3/3) and 30 percent brown (10YR 4/3) silty clay loam; moderate fine subangular blocky structure; friable; common very fine and fine roots throughout; common fine interstitial pores; 10 percent prominent light gray (10YR 7/2), dry, silt coats on all faces of peds and 10 percent faint dark brown (10YR 3/3), clay films on all faces of peds; neutral (pH 7.1); clear smooth boundary

 2Bt2--43 to 60 centimeters; 70 percent brown (10YR 4/3) and 30 percent dark yellowish brown (10YR 4/4) silty clay loam; moderate medium subangular blocky and moderate coarse subangular blocky structure; firm; common very fine and fine roots throughout; common very fine vesicular and interstitial pores, and common fine dendritic tubular pores; 5 percent prominent light gray (10YR 7/2), dry, silt coats on all faces of peds and 10 percent faint dark brown (10YR 3/3) clay films on all faces of peds; 2 percent fine distinct black (10YR 2/1) manganese masses and 2 percent fine distinct brown (10YR 5/3) iron depletions; neutral (pH 7.0); clear smooth boundary

 2Bt3--60 to 95 centimeters; 80 percent dark yellowish brown (10YR 4/4) and 20 percent brown (10YR 4/3) silty clay loam; weak coarse subangular blocky structure; firm; common very fine roots throughout; common very fine vesicular and fine dendritic tubular pores; 5 percent faint dark brown (10YR 3/3) clay films on all faces of peds; 5 percent fine distinct grayish brown (10YR 5/2) iron depletions; neutral (pH 6.9); clear smooth boundary

 2Bt4--95 to 135 centimeters; dark yellowish brown (10YR 4/4) silt loam; weak coarse subangular blocky structure; friable; common very fine roots throughout; common very fine vesicular and few fine dendritic tubular pores; 8 percent fine distinct grayish brown (10YR 5/2) iron depletions; neutral (pH 6.6); gradual smooth boundary (combined thickness of the 2Bt horizons is 90 to 160 centimeters thick)

 2C--135 to 200 centimeters; brown (10YR 4/3) silt loam; massive; friable; few very fine roots throughout; few very fine vesicular pores; 10 percent medium faint grayish brown (10YR 5/2), iron depletions; slightly acid (pH 6.5)

COMPETING SERIES: These are the Baraboo, Birkbeck, Cadiz, Campton, Elco, Eleroy, Inton, Iona, Libre, Mayville, Minnith, Morningsun, Redbud, Rocheport, Rockfield, Somonauk, Uniontown, Winfield, and Zurich soils, all of which lack human-transported material near the surface. 

Baraboo, Eleroy, and Rocheport soils have a paralithic or lithic contact.
Birkbeck soils have E horizons and carbonates as high as 102 centimeters from the surface.
Campton soils have carbonates and textures with more than 20 percent sand below the particle-size control section.
Cadiz soil have more than 5 percent gravel in the particle-size control section and carbonates as high as 61 centimeters from the surface.  
Elco soils have E horizons and more than 15 percent sand in the lower parts of the argillic.
Inton soils have chromas of 2 or less in the lower parts of the argillic and lack a lithologic discontinuity. 
Iona soils have carbonates as high as 102 centimeters from the surface and lack a lithologic discontinuity.
Libre and Rockfield soils have a densic contact.
Mayville soils have carbonates as high as 51 centimeters from the surface and more than 15 percent sand below the particle-size control section. 
Minnith soils have E horizons and more than 15 percent sand below the particle-size control section.
Morningsun soils have have carbonates as high as 102 centimeters from the surface and more than 15 percent sand below the particle-size control section. 
Redbud soils have E horizons, up to 55 percent clay in the lower parts of the argillic, and prismatic structure throughout the particle-size control section.
Somonauk soils have between 15 and 75 percent sand in the lower parts of the argillic.
Uniontown soils have carbonate concretions below the particle=size control section and lack a lithologic discontinuity.
Winfield soils lack a lithologic discontinuity, are very strongly acid to slightly acid in the lower part of the argillic and have E horizons.
Zurich soils have more than 15 percent sand in the lower parts of the argillic and stratified textures below the argillic.

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING:
Parent material: Human-transported material over Peoria loess
Landscape: Loess hills
Landform: Interfluves, ridges, shoulders, and backslopes
Anthropogenic landform: Leveled land and fills
Slope: 2 to 30 percent
Elevation: 120 to 250 meters
Frost-free period: 184 to 228 days
Mean annual temperature: 11 to 14 degrees C
Mean annual precipitation: 940 to 1194 millimeters

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: These are the Fishpot, Harvester, Shaw, and Vandeventer soils which have mantles of human-transported material greater than 50 centimeters thick, and the competing Windfield soils. 

Fishpot soils formed in alluvial materials and are subject to frequent flooding on nearby fills on stream terraces and floodplains.
Harvester soils have aquic conditions beginning below 55 centimeters, have buried argillic horizons, and are found on similar landforms.
Shaw soils have mollic epipedons, aquic conditions within 40 centimeters, and are on nearby leveled or filled interfluves.
Vandeventer soils have between 35 and 80 percent human artifacts in the particle-size control section and are found on similar landforms.
Winfield soils are found on nearby unmodified loess hills.

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses are for recreational and residential applications in an urban setting. Vegetation may include managed and unmanaged plant communities with a mix of native and non-native species. Many areas are vegetated with non-native ornamental landscaping or turfgrass. Some areas exhibit weedy, invasive, and primary succession vegetation communities (e.g., abandoned green space and other unmanaged areas). 

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT:
Urban and other areas with human-modified landscapes in the Central Feed Grains and Livestock Region, Major Land Resource Area 115. These soils are of moderate extent.
