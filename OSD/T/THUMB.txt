Slope: 0 to 2 percent

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Sandy, mixed Typic Cryaquents

TYPICAL PEDON: Thumb sand in an estuarine herbaceous community on a linear, linear beach plain on a slope of 1 percent at an elevation of 7 m (23 ft).

     Oe--0 to 10 cm (0 to 4 in); moderately decomposed plant material, black (10YR 2/1) moist, dark gray (10YR 4/1) dry; many very fine roots throughout and many very fine roots throughout and many medium roots throughout; slightly acid (pH 6.4); clear smooth boundary.

     C1--10 to 40 cm (4 to 16 in); sand, very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) moist, grayish brown (10YR 5/2) dry; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine roots throughout and common medium roots throughout and common fine roots throughout; neutral (pH 6.9); clear smooth boundary.

     Cg1--40 to 60 cm (16 to 24 in); loamy sand, very dark gray (5Y 3/1) moist, gray (5Y 5/1) dry; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; common very fine roots throughout; neutral (pH 6.6); clear smooth boundary.

     Cg2--60 to 80 cm (24 to 32 in); sandy clay loam, greenish gray (10BG 5/1) moist, light greenish gray (10BG 7/1) dry; weak medium platy structure; firm, nonsticky, nonplastic; slightly acid (pH 6.3); clear smooth boundary.

     C'--80 to 200 cm (32 to 79 in); extremely gravelly sand, dark gray (2.5Y 4/1) moist, gray (2.5Y 6/1) dry; structureless single grain; 80 percent rounded gravels by volume; neutral (pH 6.6); clear smooth boundary.

COMPETING SERIES: These are the Abyss (AK), Ashmun (AK), Chilkoot (AK), Monashka (AK), Perouse (AK), Stave (AK), and Topsy (AK) series.
Abyss soils have pHs ranging to slightly alkaline and occur on high flood plains and valleys of drift plains.
Ashmun soils have high elevations values ranging to 305 m (1000 ft) and are very poorly drained.
Chilkoot soils have high elevations values ranging to 244 m (800 ft) and a mean annual air temperature of 4.5 to 7 degrees C (40 to 45 degrees F).
Monashka soils are very poorly drained and have a means annual precipitation of 2150 to 6400 mm (85 to 252 in).
Stave soils have a poorly drained drainage class.
Topsy soils have a poorly drained drainage class.
