COWDREY SERIES

The Cowdrey series consists of very deep, well drained soils formed in colluvium from glacial till, glacial drift, or igneous, metamorphic, or sedimentary rock.  These soils are on mountain slopes, ridges, fans, toes, landslides, and old glacial plains. Slopes are 2 to 65 percent.  The mean annual precipitation is about 575 mm and the mean annual temperature is about 3.0 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Fine, smectitic Eutric Glossocryalfs

TYPICAL PEDON: Cowdrey clay loam in forestland.  (Colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted.)

    Oi--0 to 8 cm; slightly decomposed organic material, mainly needles, bark, and twigs.

    Oe--8 to 10 cm; partially decomposed organic material. 

    A--10 to 18 cm; very dark gray (10YR 3/1) clay loam, black (10YR 2/1) moist; strong fine granular structure; soft, very friable, 5 percent gravel and cobble[RC-NIFI1.1]; neutral (pH 6.6); abrupt wavy boundary.  

    E--18 to 30 cm; light gray (10YR 7/2) clay loam, grayish brown (10YR 5/2) moist; moderate medium platy structure parting to strong fine granular; soft, very friable; 10 percent gravel and cobbles; slightly acid (pH 6.2); gradual wavy boundary.  

    E/B--30 to 40 cm; mixed[RC-NIFI2.1] light gray (10YR 7/2) and brown (10YR 5/3) clay loam, mixed grayish brown (10YR 5/2) and brown (10YR 4/3) moist; strong fine angular blocky structure; very hard, very friable; few continuous distinct clay films on ped faces; slightly acid (pH 6.4); clear smooth boundary.  

    Bt1--40 to 71 cm; brown (10YR 5/3) clay, brown (10YR 4/3) strong medium angular blocky structure; extremely hard, friable, very plastic; continuous clay films on rock fragments; 14 percent gravel; neutral (pH 6.6); gradual wavy boundary.  

    Bt2--71 to 86 cm; light olive brown (2.5Y 5/4) gravelly clay, olive brown (2.5Y 4/4) moist; weak medium angular blocky structure; very hard, firm, very plastic; extremely hard; many discontinuous distinct clay films on faces of peds; few discontinuous faint clay films on surfaces along root channels and on rock fragments; 25 percent gravel; neutral (pH 6.8); diffuse wavy boundary.  

    C--86 to 163 cm; light olive gray (5Y 6/2) clay, olive gray (5Y 5/2) moist; massive; extremely hard, very plastic; 14 percent gravel and cobble; neutral (ph 7.0).

TYPE LOCATION: Jackson County, Colorado; 740 feet south of the center of Sec. 24, T. 7 N., R. 77 W.[RC-NIFI3.1]

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING:  
Landform--mountain slopes, ridges, fans, toes, landslides, and old glacial plains
Elevation--2,130 to 3,600 meters
Slope--2 to 65 percent
Parent material--colluvium from glacial till, glacial drift, or igneous, metamorphic, or sedimentary rock
Climate--long, cold winters; cool, moist springs; short, cool summers 
Mean annual precipitation--400 mm to 850 mm
Mean annual air temperature--0 to 5.0 degrees C
Frost-free period--20 to 70 days

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS: None listed.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: Well drained, 0.5 TO 50 um/sec in upper mineral horizons, 1.5 to 100 um/sec in the C horizon.

USE AND VEGETATION: Cowdrey soils are primarily used for grazing, timber and recreation.  The potential native vegetation is Douglas-fir, subalpine fir, huckleberry, heartleaf arnica, and Geyer's sedge.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: Cowdrey soils are of moderate extent in Colorado, Wyoming, and southeastern Idaho; MLRAs 48A and 43B.

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: Bozeman, Montana

SERIES ESTABLISHED: Star Valley Area, Idaho and Wyoming; 1971.

Cowdrey soils have a cryic temperature regime and an udic moisture regime.  

2/2004 The concept of the series was narrowed to typic-udic moisture to match the type location and most areas where this series is mapped.  Therefore, the classification was changed from Ustic to Eutric Glossocryalf.
