CAHUILLA SERIES

The Cahuilla series consists of very shallow to shallow, well drained soils that are formed in sandy alluvium over clayey sediments. Cahuilla soils are on hills and swales in badlands with slopes 0 to 5 percent. The mean annual precipitation is 105 mm and the mean annual air temperature is about 23 degrees C.

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Mixed, hyperthermic, shallow Typic Torripsamments

TYPICAL PEDON: Cahuilla stratified coarse sand and loamy sand on a slope of 3 percent, elevation of 238 meters, and cover of burrobush, creosote, white rhatany, saltbush, and big galleta. (colors are for dry soil unless otherwise noted). Surface fragment cover is 40 percent gravel.

  A--0 to 10 cm; pale brown (10YR 6/3) stratified coarse sand and loamy sand, brown (10YR 5/3) moist; moderate thick platy structure; very friable, slightly hard, nonsticky, nonplastic; few fine and common very fine roots, common very fine irregular pores; moderately alkaline (pH 8.0); strongly effervescent; abrupt irregular boundary. (1 to 10 cm thick)

  C--10 to 18 cm; light yellowish brown (10YR 6/4) sand, brown (10YR 5/3) moist; single grain; loose, nonsticky, nonplastic; few fine and common very fine roots, many interstitial pores; moderately alkaline (pH 8.2); strongly effervescent; abrupt irregular boundary.  (8 to 49 cm thick)

  2Cd--18 to 165 cm; clay loam, brown (10YR 4/3) moist; massive; very firm, very hard, moderately sticky, very plastic; few very fine roots in the upper part of the horizon and in vertical cracks; strongly alkaline (pH 8.6); violently effervescent. (17 to 132 cm thick)

TYPE LOCATION: San Diego County, California; approximately 732 meters (2400 feet) south and west from the Short Wash intersection with Fonts Wash; 33.2775056 degrees latitude, -116.2258917 degrees longitude WGS84; UTM Zone 11, 3682319N, 572087E. USGS Quad Name: Fonts Point, California.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:

Vint: are somewhat excessively drained, have a sandy particle-size control section, and are found on flood plains

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY: somewhat excessively to well drained; high to very high saturated hydraulic conductivity

USE AND VEGETATION: This soil is used for recreation and wildlife habitat. Vegetation is dominantly burrobush, big galleta, white bursage, creosote, desert trumpet, desert poppies, Asian mustard, ocotillo, golden cholla, and saltbush.

DISTRIBUTION AND EXTENT: The soils are of small extent and are mapped in the lower Colorado Desert of California. MLRA 40

SOIL SURVEY REGIONAL OFFICE (SSRO) RESPONSIBLE: DAVIS, CALIFORNIA

SERIES ESTABLISHED: San Diego County, California, Anza-Borrego Area Soil Survey, 2011. The soil is named after prehistoric Lake Cahuilla.
