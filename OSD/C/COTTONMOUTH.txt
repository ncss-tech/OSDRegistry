MLRA(s)--152A
Depth class--Very deep
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Parent material--Herbaceous organic material over loamy estuarine deposits

Slope range--0 to 1 percent

Mean annual precipitation--1397 millimeters (55 inches)

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Loamy, mixed, euic, hyperthermic Terric Sulfihemists

TYPICAL PEDON: Cottonmouth mucky peat in an oligohaline marsh at sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Oese--0 to 61 centimeters; very dark grayish brown (2.5Y 3/2) broken face and very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) rubbed mucky peat; 25 percent fibers, 20 percent rubbed; von Post H6; many very fine, fine, and common medium roots; slight sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.1 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.0), extremely acid (pH 3.8) oxidized pH; clear boundary (40 to 129 centimeters thick)

 Aseg--61 to 108 centimeters; very dark gray (2.5Y 3/1) mucky loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; very fluid; many very fine and common fine roots; strong sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (1.9 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.3), very strongly acid (pH 4.5) oxidized pH; clear boundary (10 to 50 centimeters thick)

 Cseg1--108 to 147 centimeters; dark gray (10YR 4/1) sandy clay loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; moderately fluid; strong sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (1.4 dS/m); slightly alkaline (pH 7.6), strongly acid (pH 5.2) oxidized pH; gradual boundary

 Cseg2--147 to 200 centimeters; 90 percent very dark grayish brown (2.5Y 3/2) and 10 percent very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) fine sandy loam; massive; slightly sticky, nonplastic; slightly fluid; slight sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; nonsaline (0.9 dS/m); slightly alkaline (pH 7.7), very strongly acid (pH 4.8) oxidized pH

TYPE LOCATION: Franklin County, Florida; Scipio Creek; approximately 2.0 kilometers (1.2 miles) east of the intersection of Hathcock Road and Linden Road, and approximately 1.3 kilometers (0.8 miles) south of Four Tree Cutoff channel which connects the St. Marks River to the Apalachicola River; USGS topographic quadrangle: West Pass.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Fushatchee, Haulover, and Millender soils.
Fushatchee soils--have greater thickness of muck or sapric soil materials compared to mucky peat or hemic soil materials and generally occur in lower salinity portions of the estuary.
Haulover soils--formed in fine-loamy estuarine deposits and have histic epipedons.
Millender soils--formed in fine-loamy estuarine deposits and do not contain hypersulfidic materials within 50 centimeters of the mineral soil surface.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Very low to moderately low
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Very frequent, for brief durations. Cottonmouth soils are flooded by wind and lunar tides, and riverine flooding. Inundation depths on Cottonmouth soils are irregular. 

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Cottonmouth soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Cottonmouth soils are typically dominated by black needlerush (Juncus roemerianus), sawgrass (Cladium jamaicense), and cosmopolitan bullrush (Bolboschoenus maritimus).

REMARKS:
The series is named after cottonmouth snakes (Agkistrodon piscivorus) observed while describing these soils. Cottonmouth soils were previously mapped as the Bohicket and Tisonia soils in Florida sol surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Hemic soil materials--The zone from 0 to 61 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is greater than one-sixth but less than three-fourths of  the original soil volume.
Hypersulfidic materials--The zone from 0 to 61 centimeters has an initial pH that decreases by 1 or more units to a value of 4.0 or less after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Hyposulfidic materials--The zone from 61 to 200 centimeters has an initial pH that decreased by 1 or more units but remains at a value greater than 4.0 after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Fluidity--The zone from 61 to 200 centimeters has a fluidity class of slightly fluid to very fluid.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
