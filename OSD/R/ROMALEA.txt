ROMALEA SERIES

MLRA(s)--152A
Depth class--Very deep
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Parent material--Fine-loamy estuarine and alluvial deposits
Geomorphic location--Freshwater and oligohaline tidal marshes
Slope range--0 to 1 percent
Mean annual air temperature--21 degrees C (70 degrees F)
Mean annual precipitation--1397 millimeters (55 inches)

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Fine-loamy, mixed, active, nonacid, hyperthermic Typic Hydraquents

TYPICAL PEDON: Romalea mucky peat in an oligohaline marsh slightly above sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Oese--0 to 11 centimeters; black (7.5YR 2.5/1) broken face and very dark brown (10YR 2/2) rubbed mucky peat; 50 percent fibers, 35 percent rubbed; von Post H4; many very fine, fine, and medium roots; no sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; strong effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; slightly saline (4.5 dS/m); moderately acid (pH 5.9), moderately acid (pH 5.8) oxidized pH; clear boundary (0 to 35 centimeters thick)

 Aseg1--11 to 27 centimeters; very dark grayish brown (2.5Y 3/2) mucky loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; very fluid; many very fine, fine, and medium roots; no sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; very slightly saline (3.5 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.1), strongly acid (pH 5.2) oxidized pH; clear boundary

 Aseg2--27 to 63 centimeters; very dark brown (10YR 2/2) mucky clay loam; massive; moderately sticky, moderately plastic; moderately fluid; many very fine, fine, and medium roots; slight sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderately saline (11.5 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.5), strongly acid (pH 5.3) oxidized pH; clear boundary (combined thickness of A horizons is 15 to 55 centimeters)

 Cseg1--63 to 169 centimeters; black (2.5Y 2.5/1) loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; very fluid; common very fine and fine, and many medium roots; slight sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderately saline (13.9 dS/m[JK1.1][AW1.2]); slightly acid (pH 6.3), slightly acid (pH 6.1) oxidized pH; clear boundary

 Cseg2--169 to 200 centimeters; olive gray (5Y 4/2) loam; massive; moderately sticky, moderately plastic; moderately fluid; few fine and common medium roots; 15 percent fine mica flakes; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; strong effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; very slightly saline (3.6 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.1), slightly acid (pH 6.1) oxidized pH

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Maurepas, Opilwa, and Yent soils.
Maurepas soils--formed in woody organic material and have 130 centimeters or greater of organic soil materials at the soil surface. 
Opilwa soils--formed in fine-loamy alluvium over woody organic materials and contain 40 centimeters or more of buried organic soil material. 
Yent soils--formed in herbaceous organics over loamy alluvium and estuarine deposits and have over 40 centimeters of sapric material at the soil surface. 

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Moderately high to high
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Frequent or very frequent, for long durations. These soils are flooded by wind and lunar tides, or overbank flow. Inundation depths on Romalea soils are irregular, but flooding is consistent. 

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Romalea soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Romalea soils are typically dominated by black needlerush (Juncus roemerianus), sawgrass (Cladium jamaicense), and cosmopolitan bullrush (Bolboschoenus maritimus). Along areas adjacent to major floodplains, cottonmouth grass is widespread (Phanopyrum gymnocarpon) and limited stands of white tupelo (Nyssa ogeche) and bald cypress (Taxodium distichum) are established.

REMARKS:
The series is named after Eastern lubber grasshoppers (Romalea microptera) observed while describing these soils. Some Romalea soils that exist in lower flow areas may exhibit increased salinity in the substratum due to oceanic salt wedges penetrating the estuary, or retention of salts by the cation exchange capacity after tropical storm surge. However, the exchangeable sodium percent and sodium adsorption ratio are not consistently[RT2.1] high enough to meet the classification for Sodic Hydraquents. Romalea soils were previously mapped as the Bayvi, Brickyard, Chowan, Dirego, Kenner, Maurepas soils in Florida soil surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Ochric epipedon--The zone from 0 to 63 centimeters
Hemic soil materials--The zone from 0 to 11 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is greater than one-sixth but less than three-fourths of  the original soil volume.
Fluidity--The zone from 11 to 200 centimeters has a fluidity class of moderately fluid to very fluid.
Hyposulfidic materials--The zone from 27 to 63 centimeters has an initial pH that decreases by 1 or more units but remains at a value greater than 4.0 after 16 weeks of moist aerobic incubation.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
