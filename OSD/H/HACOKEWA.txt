Depth class--Very deep
Drainage class--Very poorly drained

Geomorphic location--Polyhaline tidal marshes
Slope range--0 to 1 percent
Mean annual air temperature--21 degrees C (70 degrees F)
Mean annual precipitation--1397 millimeters (55 inches)

TAXONOMIC CLASS: Loamy, mixed, euic, hyperthermic Terric Haplosaprists

TYPICAL PEDON: Hacokewa muck in a polyhaline marsh on a barrier island, 0.3 meter above sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Oase--0 to 53 centimeters; very dark brown (10YR 2/2) broken face rubbed muck; 35 percent fibers, 12 percent rubbed; von Post H7; many very fine and fine, and common medium roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderately saline (10.2 dS/m); neutral (pH 6.6), slightly acid (pH 6.4) oxidized pH; clear boundary (40 to 120 centimeters thick)

 Cseg1--53 to 94 centimeters; dark greenish gray (10Y 4/1) clay loam; massive; moderately sticky, moderately plastic; very fluid; common very fine, few fine and medium roots; 8 percent faint very dark gray (2.5Y 3/1) organic stains on ped faces; moderate sulfurous odor; color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; strongly saline (16.4 dS/m); neutral (pH 6.8), slightly acid (pH 6.4) oxidized pH; clear boundary

 Cseg2--94 to 200 centimeters; very dark gray (2.5Y 3/1) sandy loam; massive; slightly sticky, slightly plastic; moderately fluid; few very fine and fine roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderate effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderately saline (14.3 dS/m); slightly alkaline (pH 7.4), neutral (pH 6.8) oxidized pH

TYPE LOCATION: Franklin County, Florida; St. Vincent Island; approximately 5.9 kilometers (3.7 miles) north of West Pass, and approximately 16.6 kilometers (10.3 miles) east of the intersection of Barefoot Trail and County Road 30A in Gulf County, FL; USGS topographic quadrangle: West Pass

COMPETING SERIES:
These are the Delcomb and Yent soils
Delcomb soils--formed in herbaceous organic material over silty sediments on brackish coastal marshes, do not have recorded sulfurous odors, and are slightly finer textured in their substratum layers.
Yent soils--formed in herbaceous and woody organic material over loamy estuarine deposits in freshwater and oligohaline tidal marshes. 

GEOGRAPHIC SETTING:

Slope--0 to 1 percent

Water salinity range--18 to 30 parts per thousand
Frost free period--330 to 350 days

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Decuevas, Hoyana, Lahnee, and Pompano soils.
Decuevas soils--formed in fine-loamy lagoonal and marine deposits and do not have more than 40 centimeters of organic soil material starting at the soil surface.
Hoyana soils--formed in coarse-loamy lagoonal deposits and do not have more than 40 centimeters of organic soil material starting at the soil surface.
Lahnee soils--formed in fine-loamy lagoonal deposits, do not have more than 40 centimeters of organic soil material starting at the soil surface, and have hues of 10Y to 5BG in C horizons. 
Pompano soils--formed in thick beds of sandy marine sediments and are poorly drained.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Very low to moderately low
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Very frequent, for very brief duration by lunar tides.  

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Hacokewa soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Black needlerush (Juncus roemerianus), smooth cordgrass (Spartina alterniflora), saltgrass (Distichlis spicata), and Sea purslane (Sesuvium portulacastrum). Black needlerush on Hacokewa soils typically exhibits high salinity stress and is less than one meter tall.

REMARKS:
The series is named the anglicized word for "osprey" (Pandion haliaetus) in Muscogee Creek, Hacokewa. Hacokewa soils were previously mapped as Bohicket and Tisonia soil series in Florida soil surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Sapric soil materials--The zone from 0 to 53 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is less than one-sixth of the original soil volume.
Fluidity--The zone from 53 to 200 centimeters has a fluidity class of moderately fluid to very fluid.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
