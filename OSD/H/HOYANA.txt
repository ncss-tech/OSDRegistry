TAXONOMIC CLASS: Coarse-loamy, mixed, semiactive, nonacid, hyperthermic Sodic Hydraquents

TYPICAL PEDON: Hoyana mucky peat in a polyhaline marsh at 0.6 meters above sea level (colors are for moist soil unless otherwise stated; electrical conductivity is 1:1 by volume solution mixture unless otherwise stated).

 Oese--0 to 23 centimeters; very dark grayish brown (10YR 3/2) and very dark brown (10YR 2/2) rubbed mucky peat; 60 percent fibers, 30 percent rubbed; von Post H5; many very fine and fine, and few coarse roots; moderate sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; slightly saline (4.8 dS/m); slightly acid (pH 6.3), moderately acid (pH 5.7) oxidized pH; gradual boundary (0 to 25 centimeters thick)

 Aseg--23 to 43 centimeters; black (2.5Y 2.5/1) loam; massive; nonsticky, nonplastic; very fluid; common fine roots; slight sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderately saline (12.4 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.2), neutral (pH 7.2) oxidized pH; clear boundary (20 to 55 centimeters thick)

 Cseg1--43 to 93 centimeters; dark gray (N 4/) sandy loam; massive; nonsticky, nonplastic; moderately fluid; slight sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; strongly saline (16.1 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.0), slightly acid (pH 6.3) oxidized pH; gradual boundary

 Cseg2--93 to 200 centimeters; dark gray (2.5Y 4/1) sandy loam; massive; nonsticky, nonplastic; slightly fluid; strong sulfurous odor; no color change with 3 percent hydrogen peroxide; slight effervescence with 30 percent hydrogen peroxide; moderately saline (15.2 dS/m); neutral (pH 7.1), neutral (pH 6.7) oxidized pH

TYPE LOCATION: Franklin County, Florida; St. Vincent National Wildlife Refuge; approximately 0.3 kilometers (0.2 miles) south of the intersection of I Road and 5 Road on St. Vincent National Wildlife Refuge, and approximately 5.0 kilometers (3.1 miles) west of St. Vincent Point; USGS topographic quadrangle: Indian Pass.

GEOGRAPHICALLY ASSOCIATED SOILS:
These are the Decuevas, Hacokewa, Lahnee, and Pompano soils.
Decuevas soils--formed in fine-loamy lagoonal and marine deposits on similar landforms.
Hacokewa soils--formed in herbaceous organic material over loamy lagoonal deposits and have greater than 40 centimeters of soil organic material.
Lahnee soils--formed in fine-loamy lagoonal deposits and have matrix colors greener than 10Y in some part of the soil below the mineral surface. 
Pompano soils--formed in thick beds of sandy marine sediments and are poorly drained.

DRAINAGE AND SATURATED HYDRAULIC CONDUCTIVITY:
Drainage class--Very poorly drained
Saturated hydraulic conductivity--Moderately high to high
Soil moisture regime--Peraquic
Flooding--Very frequent, for very brief durations. Flooded by lunar tides

USE AND VEGETATION:
Major uses--Hoyana soils are used mainly as wetland wildlife habitat.
Typical vegetation--Black needlerush (Juncus roemerianus), smooth cordgrass (Spartina alterniflora), saltgrass (Distichlis spicata), and Sea purslane (Sesuvium portulacastrum). Black needlerush on Hoyana soils typically exhibit high salinity stress and is less than one meter tall.

REMARKS:
The series is named after the anglicized spelling of the Muscogee Creek word for "hurricane". Hoyana soils were previously mapped as the Bayvi, Bohicket, Dirego, and Tisonia soil series in Florida soil surveys.

Diagnostic horizons and other diagnostic soil characteristics recognized in this pedon are (Keys to Soil Taxonomy, Thirteenth Edition, 2022):
Hemic soil materials--The zone from 0 to 23 centimeters has a fiber content after rubbing that is greater than one-sixth but less than three-fourths of  the original soil volume.
Histic epipedon--The zone from 0 to 23 centimeters.  The histic epipedon is not recognized taxonomically as the "sodic" subgroup keys out first and the "histic" subgroup is not a choice as well.
Fluidity--The zone from 23 to 200 centimeters has a fluidity class of slightly fluid to very fluid.
Peraquic feature--Zero or positive water potential at or near the soil surface.
